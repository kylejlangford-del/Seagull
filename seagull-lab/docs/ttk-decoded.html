TTK, decoded

How the number on the sailors' screen is actually computed — reverse-engineered from 12,000 pre-start samples across all fifty races in the archive — and what TTK BOAT does with it.

What the onboard computer does

The logged channels obey one identity everywhere: ratio = time you have / time you need and TTK = time you have − time you need, floored at −100. Everything interesting is in "time you need", and fitting it across the archive settles what the model is:

  • The target is the nearest point of the line — the perpendicular foot, clamped to an end when you are off an end (which is most of the entry). Confirmed against 6,500 off-end samples: a time built to the near end fits; one built to the perpendicular or the far end does not.
  • The run accelerates. Within one wind band, the implied speed rises with the speed you are already doing at a slope of about a third — the signature of a model that builds from current speed toward a target, not of one that teleports you to the target. A boat doing 20 km/h is credited with far less line than a boat doing 60 at the same range.
  • The acceleration is one constant per rig config, and the target speed follows the polar for the angle of the run — high at a reach, collapsing to ~25 km/h dead downwind. The fitted constants:

Does the physics hold up?

Two scorecards. First, recomputing "time you need" both ways against the onboard channels; second — the one that cannot be faked — against real crossings: a boat that crossed the line late had nothing left to kill, so its TTK in the final approach is a prediction of exactly how late it will be.

The tool's previous TO KILL ran on best-VMC with no acceleration — about 5 seconds optimistic against what boats actually did, always in the flattering direction. The accel model lands within two seconds of reality, with its remaining bias also optimistic (no turn cost, clean air assumed) — the right side to be wrong on, and now a small one.

TTK BOAT

The onboard TTK answers "can I make the line". It cannot answer "can I make the boat end" — on a reaching start the line is set along the wind, so the run from down-line up to the committee boat is a different angle with a different polar speed, and the two numbers diverge hard as you come down the line. TTK BOAT is the same physics aimed at the top end:

TTK BOAT = time to the gun − accelerating run to the windward end

with the run integrated on the measured per-config acceleration table (the real curve, soft patch and all — better than the constant the onboard model uses), capped at the polar for the rhumb-line angle, with the no-go treated exactly as the start cone treats it. TRIGGER in the CONE panel sets how good a pull both assume.

  • Positive — you can be at the boat end with that many seconds in hand. Time you must kill.
  • Negative (amber) — the boat end is slipping away: out of reach by that many seconds even at potential.
  • "out of reach" — the straight run to it is inside the no-go. You cannot lay the boat end on this board; it would take a tack, and no start tool should price a tack you have not decided to make.

Convention matches the cockpit: rhumb line only, one board. TO KILL (nearest point) and TTK BOAT sit side by side in MY BOAT — at Sassnitz T−20, AUS reads TO KILL +7.3 s against TTK BOAT −10.4 s: safe against the line, already too late for the top end. That gap is the decision.
