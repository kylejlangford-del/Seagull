The last tack, priced

Every pre-start in the archive, the final tack before the gun — 264 of them — and what each costs in seconds, split by rig config and the wind it was sailed in.

How it is measured

A tack's bill has two parts. The bite — entry speed down to the minimum through the turn — is read straight off the track and is unambiguous. The rebuild is not: after the last tack most boats deliberately stay slow (they are killing time), so the observed recovery mixes physics with choice. The rebuild is therefore modelled — the boat re-accelerates from its measured minimum at the per-config p75 acceleration (upwind band, capped at entry speed) — giving the cost of a tack you commit out of. Cost = bite + rebuild, in seconds lost against a boat that never tacked.

What it says

  • Wind halves the bill, twice. In the heavy-air rig above 28 km/h a committed tack costs 5–7 s. In the mid rigs under 28 it is 10–16 s, and in real light air ~15 s. Same manoeuvre, three times the price.
  • The turn is cheap; the rebuild is the bill. The bite is 1.6–4.9 s everywhere. What you pay for is re-accelerating from ~55–65% of entry speed at whatever rate the rig and the breeze allow — which is why the cost tracks the acceleration table, not the helming.
  • Everyone tacks at the same moment. The last tack sits at T−25 to T−36 in every config, later in more wind. A settled final-approach tack and a repositioning tack cost within half a second of each other — the cost is in the physics, not the intent.
  • This is the missing term in TTK. TO KILL and TTK BOAT price the straight run and say "out of reach" across the no-go. This table is what a tack actually costs — the number to add the day the tool starts quoting two-board solutions.
