Starboard sectors

Same eight wedges, same geometry — they are signed to windward, so they already flip with the tack. What changes on starboard is the rights, and therefore what each sector is worth. Both of these are now built: the tool picks the table off your own tack, live, as you tack. Each wheel is drawn as seen from above with the wind blowing down the page and the boat close-hauled at 45° to it — on port the bow leans RIGHT and the wind comes over the port side; on starboard the bow leans LEFT.

The pattern is a quarter turn. On port the danger is aft and to leeward — the hook on our transom, the boat under our bow that can luff us. On starboard it is above and behind: C and D, the boats that can bear down on us where being the rights boat buys us nothing. Everything the rights DO cover drops away — the transom, the leeward bow, both windward bows all fall to the bottom two levels.

CRITICAL flashes, on either tack. The +20° sliver is a port-tack thing only: there the critical zone runs from +20° round the transom to −45°, taking all of E and just the inner edge of D. On starboard D is critical end to end and needs no exception.
